# Must have vs good to have

Split from the original assignment and how they said they will score it.

Must-have means the product does not match the brief if it is missing.
Good-to-have means polish that helps on the call but is not required for the brief to exist.

## Must have

### Ask

- An endpoint that takes first name and last name
- Optional birth date
- Optional address, city, and country
- A hard spending ceiling for that one search

### Shop

- Pay for lookups through Perflo’s marketplace, not through a pile of your own vendor keys
- Use more than one kind of source: work and social (LinkedIn, X, Instagram), web or deep search, public records or risk data, plus anything else in the catalog that actually helps
- Cover contact and job history, social profiles and public posts, news and public records, PEP and sanctions, and fraud or scam signals

### Spend

- A cheap ceiling produces a thin file
- A larger ceiling produces a deeper file
- Total spend never goes over the ceiling

### Think

- An agent chooses which lookups to buy
- It merges the results
- It skips repeats
- It deals with conflicting facts when it can
- It writes one structured file

### Return

- Who they appear to be, and what was found
- Sources
- Possible people, each with a confidence
- PEP, sanctions, fraud, and reputation
- An overall risk call
- Every paid lookup, its cost, and the total
- Clear warnings when a person or a fact could not be verified

### Quality they will press on

- Right person, or an honest maybe (same name in another city is not a hit)
- A clear ask to shop to assemble design
- Budget discipline
- Speed (independent lookups can run together)
- Real tool-calling, not a fixed shopping list
- Readable code, docs, and failures that do not hide

## Good to have

These make it feel like a real product.
They are not what the brief requires to exist.

- A website or dashboard on top of the service
- Login or API keys for this research endpoint
- One isolated spending pot per search, so two searches cannot steal from each other
- Saving past files, or running a search in the background and polling later
- Caching so the same person is not paid for twice in a short window
- Email checks, skip-trace, and company filings on a high ceiling
- A dedicated watchlist vendor if one appears in the catalog
- Automatic failover when one vendor dies
- Tests, sample people, and a short how-to-run for the call
- Billing statements, webhooks, and kill-switch wiring beyond the one-search ceiling

Today the Perflo catalog does not list a clear PEP or sanctions product.
The must-have is still an honest risk section, plus news and web lookup, not a fake watchlist hit.

## One line

Must-have is one search in, one honest costed file out, never overspend.
Everything else is polish.
